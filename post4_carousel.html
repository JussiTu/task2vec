The 11% — what they did differently
Learning didn't happen by accident.
It happened because the work escalated.
1
Repeated work in one area
Same module, similar problems — resolution time began falling. Pattern recognition accumulating.
2
Faster — and broader
Tickets took less time AND touched more files and modules. Speed gained while scope expanded — the transfer signal.
3
Harder work assigned
Cross-cutting problems, core modules, more collaborators per ticket. The ceiling rose because the work demanded it.
The difference from the 70%: they were given progressively more demanding tickets — not just more tickets.

The 20% — how to spot it before it's too late
Forgetting is invisible until you look at the right signal
✓
Visible in sprint reviews
Tickets closed. Throughput normal. Looks like a solid contributor by every standard metric.
↘
Hidden in the complexity trend
Modules per ticket declining. Core codebase involvement shrinking. Resolution time rising even as the work gets simpler. Year after year.
The forgetting signal can be detected 2–3 years before it becomes a capability gap — if you know where to look. Most teams don't look until they need to promote someone and discover the depth isn't there.

What you can read from it
Three questions your history
can already answer
1
Who is in which trajectory?
Learning, stagnant, or forgetting — the split is readable from ticket and commit history. No survey required.
2

3
Who needs intervention now?
The forgetting signal appears years before it becomes visible to managers. Early detection changes the outcome.
